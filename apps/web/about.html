KANTA is an offline-first karaoke application for iPhone, iPad and Android, built around the music files you already own. This site explains what the project is, what has been tested, and what has not.

Getting startedSource and issue tracker

What KANTA is

KANTA plays karaoke files from your own library — MIDI and KAR songs with timed lyrics, plus MP3 and WAV backing tracks — with a singer queue so a room can take turns. It stores your library on your device and works without an account or a subscription. It ships three original starter songs and includes no commercial song catalog; the music you add stays your own.

The iOS and Android apps are developed independently in one repository and share no runtime. Features available on each platform differ; the feature matrix lists the current status of each one per platform.

What has been tested — and what has not

The downloads on this site are development previews, not app-store releases. Android emulator checks and iPhone/iPad simulator walkthroughs pass in continuous integration, and each published build records its source revision and checksum on its download page.

Physical-device acceptance — audio quality, microphone latency, cameras, and external displays — is still open, and it is stated plainly on the help page wherever it applies. We do not describe planned work as shipped, and the feature matrix marks experimental and pending items as such.

How this site is maintained

Product claims on the features, guide and help pages are checked against the application source before publication. Blog articles describe general karaoke practice and how the app works; when an article describes app behavior, it follows the same verified feature list.

The site is hosted on Vercel, the source lives on GitHub, and the sitemap and page metadata are kept current with each site release.

Contact and accountability

KANTA is maintained by its development team as an independent project. It is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any commercial karaoke network or equipment maker.

Questions, bug reports and feature requests go through the GitHub issue tracker — it is the project's public record of what is broken, what is planned and what has shipped. For how your data is handled, read the privacy policy; for music-rights questions, see music rights and licensing.
