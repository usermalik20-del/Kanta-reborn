SPICE Pitch Estimation: How On-Device Vocal Analysis Works

2026-10-22By KANTA Team18 min read

What SPICE Is

SPICE (Pitch Estimation) is Google's on-device machine learning model for estimating the fundamental frequency (pitch) of a singing voice in real time. It runs entirely on TensorFlow Lite, which means audio never leaves your device — no cloud processing, no server round-trips, no privacy exposure.

KANTA uses SPICE to power its real-time pitch display and scoring. When you sing, KANTA captures microphone input, runs it through SPICE, and shows you your pitch against the target note — instantly, locally, privately.

How the SPICE Model Works

SPICE is a lightweight convolutional neural network trained to estimate pitch from audio. Here's the pipeline:

1. Audio Capture

KANTA records microphone input at 16 kHz, mono. This is the sample rate SPICE expects — higher rates are downsampled, lower rates lose pitch resolution.

2. Preprocessing

The raw audio is converted to a log-mel spectrogram — a visual representation of frequency content over time. SPICE processes frames of about 32 ms with 10 ms hop size, so it produces a pitch estimate roughly every 10 ms (100 Hz update rate).

3. Model Inference

The TensorFlow Lite model takes the spectrogram frames and outputs:

  • Pitch — the estimated fundamental frequency in Hz
  • Confidence — how sure the model is that this frame contains a clear singing voice
  • Voice activity — whether the frame contains singing at all, vs. silence or background noise

4. Post-processing

KANTA converts Hz to musical notes (A4 = 440 Hz reference), applies smoothing to eliminate jitter, and gates low-confidence estimates so breath noise or silence doesn't jump around on screen.

Calibration: Why It Matters

Every microphone is different. Every device's audio chain has slight offsets. Without calibration, your pitch display might consistently read 20 cents sharp or flat.

KANTA includes a calibration routine:

  1. Play a reference tone (A4 = 440 Hz) through headphones
  2. Sing or hum along into the microphone
  3. KANTA measures the offset between what you sang and what the model heard
  4. Applies a correction factor to all subsequent pitch estimates

Recalibrate whenever you switch microphones, headphones, or devices. It takes 30 seconds and dramatically improves scoring accuracy.

Confidence Gating: When to Trust the Display

SPICE outputs a confidence score (0.0 to 1.0) for every frame. KANTA uses this to decide what to show you:

ConfidenceMeaningKANTA Behavior
0.8 – 1.0Clear, sustained singing voiceShow pitch note and scoring normally
0.5 – 0.8Probably voice, some noiseShow pitch with reduced opacity; exclude from scoring average
0.3 – 0.5Uncertain — could be voice or noiseFade out pitch display; don't count toward score
Below 0.3Silence, breath, background noiseHide pitch display entirely

This prevents the embarrassing scenario where coughing between songs registers as a high C and wrecks your accuracy score.

Song-Key Targeting

KANTA lets you set a target key for each song. When you load a KAR/MIDI file, KANTA knows the melody notes. SPICE listens to your voice and compares each sung pitch to the nearest target note.

The scoring pipeline:

  1. Note segmentation — KANTA splits the melody into individual notes with start times and durations
  2. Pitch tracking — SPICE estimates your pitch every 10 ms while you sing each note
  3. Cent deviation — KANTA calculates how many cents (1/100th of a semitone) you're off from the target
  4. Confidence weighting — only high-confidence frames count toward your score
  5. Timing check — did you start and end the note at the right time?

Your final score blends pitch accuracy (60%), timing (30%), and consistency (10%).

Privacy Architecture

This is the part that matters most for a singing app. Here's KANTA's privacy guarantee:

  • 100% on-device — SPICE runs via TensorFlow Lite entirely on your phone. No audio is uploaded.
  • No server round-trip — there's no "cloud scoring" mode. Even offline, pitch estimation works perfectly.
  • No audio storage — KANTA doesn't record or save your singing. The microphone buffer is processed in memory and discarded.
  • No network permission for audio — KANTA's network permission is used for app updates and companion control, never for audio transmission.

You can sing in airplane mode and get the exact same pitch display and scoring as online. Your voice stays yours.

Tips for Better Pitch Tracking

  1. Use headphones so the backing track doesn't bleed into the microphone and confuse SPICE
  2. Calibrate before each session, especially with a new microphone
  3. Sing close to the mic (6–12 inches) for strong signal-to-noise ratio
  4. Avoid vibrato at the start of notes — hold the pitch steady for the first 200 ms so SPICE can lock on
  5. Quiet environment — background TV, fans, or conversation lower confidence and create spurious readings
