Party Effects & Intermission Music: Elevating Your Karaoke Nights

2026-09-25By KANTA Team8 min read

KANTA's Eight Built-In Effects

EffectWhat It DoesBest For
ReverbAdds space and ambiance — sounds like singing in a hallBallads, emotional songs
Echo / DelayRepeats your voice at set intervalsPop, rock, dramatic endings
ApplausePlays crowd cheering between songsSet transitions, finishing a number
LaughterLighthearted crowd reactionFunny moments, between-song banter
BooPlayful negative reactionInside jokes, friendly roasts
AirhornLoud attention-grabberTransitions, hype moments
Drum RollBuilds anticipation before a songIntroducing a performer
Record ScratchSudden stop sound effectComedy, cutting off a song

Each effect has a volume control so you can balance it against your backing track and microphone.

Custom Sound Clips

Beyond the built-in effects, KANTA lets you add your own sound clips:

  • Import MP3 or WAV files into KANTA's sound clip library
  • Assign clips to buttons for one-tap playback
  • Use them for intro music, signature sounds, or inside jokes
  • Clips play through the same output as your backing track

Ideas: Your entrance theme, a “wow” crowd sound, a catchphrase, or a short instrumental sting between songs.

Intermission Music

Between singers, silence kills the energy. KANTA's intermission feature keeps the party going:

  1. Set up a playlist of upbeat tracks for between-song breaks
  2. KANTA plays intermission music automatically when no one is singing
  3. Fade-out when the next singer is ready
  4. Volume automatically ducks when someone speaks into the microphone

Choose 10–15 high-energy tracks (disco, funk, upbeat pop) so the room never goes quiet.

Building a Party Setlist

A good karaoke night has rhythm:

PhaseSong TypeEnergy
Warm-up (first 30 min)Familiar singalongs, easy keysMedium
Peak (1–2 hours)Crowd favorites, duets, high energyHigh
Wind-down (last hour)Ballads, slower songs, group numbersLow

Use KANTA's queue system to line up the next 3–5 singers so there's no dead time between performances.

Effects During Performance

  • Before the song: Drum roll to introduce the singer
  • During the song: Light reverb to sweeten the vocal
  • After the song: Applause effect for a great performance
  • Between songs: Intermission music while the next singer prepares

Keep effects subtle — they should enhance, not distract. A little reverb goes a long way; too much echo makes lyrics hard to understand.
