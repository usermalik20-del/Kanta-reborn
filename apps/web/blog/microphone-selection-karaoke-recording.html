Microphone Selection for Karaoke: Wired, USB, and Wireless Options

2026-10-01By KANTA Team12 min read

Dynamic vs Condenser vs USB

TypeHow It WorksBest ForDrawbacks
DynamicMoves a coil in a magnetic field; rugged, handles loud soundLive karaoke, loud rooms, stage monitorsLess detail, needs gain, less sensitive to quiet vocals
CondenserCharged diaphragm, needs phantom powerRecording, quiet rooms, detailPicks up background noise, fragile, needs power
USBBuilt-in audio interface, plugs into phone/computerHome karaoke, recording, simplicityLatency, limited to one device, phone compatibility varies
WirelessTransmitter/receiver, usually dynamic capsuleFreedom to move, no cablesBattery, interference, compression, latency

For karaoke with KANTA: A dynamic handheld (Shure SM58, or budget equivalent) is the default choice — it's rugged, feedback-resistant, and works everywhere. Use condenser only in quiet, treated rooms.

Wired, USB, and Wireless

Wired (XLR or 3.5mm)

Zero latency, no batteries, cheapest. Plug into your phone's headphone jack (via adapter) or a USB audio interface. Best for home karaoke.

USB Microphones

Blue Yeti, Audio-Technica ATR2500, etc. Great sound on a computer. On Android, you need USB OTG and the microphone must be class-compliant. iPhone needs Lightning/USB-C camera adapter. Can have latency issues with some apps.

Wireless

Handheld wireless (Shure GLXD, Rode Wireless ME) or lavalier. Freedom to move around. Watch out for: battery life, 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi, and slight compression. Always do a soundcheck before guests arrive.

Bluetooth Limitations

Avoid Bluetooth microphones for serious karaoke. Bluetooth audio adds 150–300 ms of latency — you'll hear your voice after you sing it, which makes staying on pitch nearly impossible. Bluetooth is fine for listening to backing tracks, but not for monitoring your voice while singing.

If you must use Bluetooth headphones for listening, use a wired microphone for singing, and route audio to wired headphones or a PA.

Using Microphones with KANTA

KANTA supports microphone input for real-time pitch display and scoring:

  1. Connect your microphone (wired via jack/adapter, USB via OTG, or the phone's built-in mic)
  2. Go to More → Settings → Microphone
  3. Select your input device
  4. Run the calibration routine
  5. Enable Microphone Monitor if you want to hear yourself through headphones
  6. Disable monitor if using speakers or PA (feedback risk)

KANTA shows your pitch in real time against the target notes from KAR/MIDI files. The better your microphone, the more accurate the display.

Feedback and Gain Staging

Feedback is that ear-piercing squeal when your microphone picks up speaker sound and loops it. Prevent it:

  • Point the microphone away from speakers
  • Keep gain as low as possible while still getting a clean signal
  • Use headphones for monitoring instead of speakers
  • Don't cup the microphone (holding the grille changes the pickup pattern and increases feedback)

Gain staging: Set your interface or mixer gain so your normal singing hits around -12 to -6 dB on KANTA's meter. Too low = noisy. Too high = distortion.
