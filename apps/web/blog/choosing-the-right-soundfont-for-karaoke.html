Choosing the Right SoundFont for Karaoke: A Complete Guide

2026-10-28By KANTA Team16 min read

What a SoundFont Actually Is

A SoundFont (.sf2 or .sf3) is a bank of recorded instrument samples packaged into a single file. When a MIDI file says "play a piano note," the SoundFont provides the actual piano sound. Without a SoundFont, MIDI playback relies on whatever generic sounds your device happens to have — which is why the same karaoke track can sound like a cheap toy on one phone and a concert hall on another.

KANTA uses TinySoundFont on Android to load SoundFonts and render MIDI/KAR tracks. The SoundFont you choose determines whether your karaoke sounds like a 1990s ringtone or a professional backing band.

GM, GS, and XG: The Three Standards

StandardOriginInstrumentsDrum KitsBest For
GM (General MIDI)1991, industry-wide128 instruments1 standard kitMaximum compatibility — every GM file plays on every GM player
GS (Roland)1991, Roland extension128+ instrumentsMultiple kitsRoland gear, richer variations, reverb/chorus control
XG (Yamaha)1994, Yamaha extension480+ instrumentsMultiple kitsYamaha gear, extensive editing, studio-quality effects

GM is the lowest common denominator. If you want a file to play identically everywhere, stick to GM instruments (channels 1–16, program 1–128). GS and XG add more instruments, drum variations, and effects parameters, but a GS file played on an XG device (or vice versa) may substitute wrong instruments or sound thin.

For karaoke, GM is usually the right choice. Most KAR and MIDI karaoke files are authored for GM, and a good GM SoundFont will make them sound correct without surprises.

Size vs Quality: When Bigger Isn't Better

SoundFonts range from 10 MB to over 2 GB. Bigger usually means more samples per instrument (multiple velocity layers, round-robin samples, longer decays), but it doesn't always mean better karaoke.

Small SoundFonts (10–100 MB):

  • Load instantly, negligible RAM usage
  • Good for budget phones and tablets
  • Single velocity layer — loud and soft notes use the same sample, just quieter/louder
  • Can sound "organ-like" or synthetic on piano and strings

Medium SoundFonts (100–500 MB):

  • The sweet spot for mobile karaoke
  • 2–4 velocity layers per instrument — soft playing sounds different from hard playing
  • Decent piano, guitar, and brass samples
  • Load time of a few seconds, acceptable memory footprint

Large SoundFonts (500 MB–2 GB+):

  • 8+ velocity layers, release samples, sympathetic resonance
  • Studio-quality piano and orchestral instruments
  • Can take 10–30 seconds to load on mobile
  • 500 MB–1 GB RAM usage — may cause crashes on low-end devices
  • Overkill for karaoke; better suited for music production

Recommendation: For KANTA on Android, aim for 150–300 MB. You get multi-sampled instruments without killing your phone's battery or memory.

How to Import a SoundFont into KANTA

  1. Download a SoundFont (.sf2) to your device's Downloads folder.
  2. Open KANTA → More → Settings → SoundFont.
  3. Tap Choose SoundFont and navigate to your downloaded file.
  4. KANTA will load it and preview a piano chord to confirm it works.
  5. All your existing MIDI/KAR tracks will immediately use the new instruments.

You can store multiple SoundFonts and switch between them depending on the song — a bright SoundFont for pop, a warm one for ballads.
