TinySoundFont: The Android MIDI Engine Powering KANTA

2026-09-28By KANTA Team17 min read

What TinySoundFont Is

TinySoundFont is an open-source C++ library that renders SoundFont (.sf2) banks into audio. It's designed to be small, fast, and efficient — exactly what you need for MIDI playback on mobile devices.

KANTA uses TinySoundFont on Android as its MIDI engine. When you load a KAR or MIDI file, TinySoundFont reads the SoundFont bank, renders the instrument samples, and produces the audio you hear — all on-device, all offline.

FluidSynth is the more famous option, but it's heavy. On Android, every megabyte of RAM and every millisecond of startup time matters. TinySoundFont gives KANTA fast app launch, quick song loading, and smooth performance on budget devices.

Performance Optimizations

KANTA's TinySoundFont integration includes:

  • Streaming sample loading — Large SoundFonts are streamed from storage rather than loaded entirely into RAM
  • Polyphony management — Caps simultaneous voices (default 64) so complex arrangements don't choke the CPU
  • Sample rate matching — Renders at the device's native output rate (44.1 kHz or 48 kHz) to avoid resampling artifacts
  • Low-latency audio path — Uses AAudio on Android 8+ for minimal audio lag
  • Background suspension — Audio engine pauses when the app is backgrounded, resumes instantly on return

Polyphony Management

Polyphony is how many notes can sound at once. A piano with sustain pedal can easily use 20–30 voices. A full backing track with drums, bass, guitar, and keys can use 50+.

KANTA defaults to 64-voice polyphony — enough for virtually any karaoke track. If you notice notes cutting out on complex arrangements, you can raise it in settings (at the cost of more CPU). On very low-end devices, dropping to 32 voices can prevent stuttering.

SoundFont Compatibility

TinySoundFont supports SF2 (SoundFont 2.04), the most common format. It reads:

  • Instrument samples (PCM, 8/16-bit)
  • Velocity layers (different samples for soft vs hard playing)
  • Key ranges (different samples across the keyboard)
  • Envelope and filter parameters (basic)
  • Reverb and chorus effects (basic)

It does not support SF3 (compressed SoundFont) or DLS. If you have an SF3 file, convert it to SF2 first using a tool like Polyphone (free, cross-platform).

Why It Matters for You

As a KANTA user, TinySoundFont means:

  • Fast startup — KANTA opens in seconds, not minutes
  • Works on budget phones — No flagship required for good MIDI sound
  • Offline playback — No internet needed to play KAR/MIDI files
  • Consistent sound — Same SoundFont, same sound on every Android device
  • Battery-friendly — Efficient rendering means longer singing sessions

Pair it with a good SoundFont (FluidR3_GM, ~140 MB) and you have a karaoke MIDI engine that rivals dedicated hardware — in an app that fits in your pocket.
