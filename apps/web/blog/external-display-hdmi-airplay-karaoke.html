External Displays for Karaoke: HDMI, AirPlay, and Wireless Options

2026-10-04By KANTA Team11 min read

Why a Dedicated Karaoke Stage

Singing from your phone is fine for practice. For a real karaoke night, you want the lyrics on a TV or projector, the audio through a proper sound system, and your phone free to control the show. KANTA's dual-screen mode does exactly this: your phone is the remote, the big screen is the stage.

Wired Options: HDMI and USB-C

ConnectionDevicesProsCons
USB-C to HDMIMost modern Android phonesLow latency, no Wi-Fi needed, charges while connectedCable limits phone placement, needs adapter
Lightning to HDMIiPhone / iPadReliable, works with any TVExpensive adapter, Lightning port occupied
Mini DisplayPort / USB-CiPad Pro, some tabletsHigh resolution, low latencyAdapter required

Tips: Use a short, high-quality HDMI cable (under 6 feet). Long cables can cause signal degradation and lip-sync issues. If your TV supports it, enable “Game Mode” or “PC Mode” to reduce input lag.

Wireless Options: AirPlay and Chromecast

WirelessDevicesProsCons
AirPlayiPhone, iPad → Apple TV, AirPlay TVsNo cables, easy setupSlight latency (200–500 ms), Wi-Fi dependent
ChromecastAndroid → Chromecast TVsNo cables, works with most AndroidLatency, Wi-Fi dependent, compression
MiracastAndroid → Miracast displaysDirect device-to-display, no routerInconsistent support, often unstable

Latency warning: Wireless introduces 200–500 ms of delay. For lyrics display alone, that's fine. If you're using the TV's speakers for audio, lipsync may drift. Connect audio separately (aux or Bluetooth to a speaker) for best results.

KANTA's Dual-Screen Mode

When you connect an external display, KANTA detects it automatically:

  1. Phone screen: Song list, queue, pitch display, tempo/key controls, microphone monitor
  2. External screen: Full-screen lyrics, large enough to read across the room, background visuals, current song progress
  3. Audio: Routes to external speakers (HDMI) or stays on phone (headphones for monitoring)

The external display shows only what an audience should see — lyrics and visuals. Your phone keeps the controls private.

Latency: The Real Challenge

Latency is the difference between when you sing and when you hear it. For karaoke, anything over 100 ms is noticeable, and over 200 ms makes singing difficult.

SetupTypical LatencyKaraoke Viable?
Phone speakers + built-in mic50–100 msYes
HDMI to TV, audio from TV100–200 msMarginal
AirPlay / Chromecast video200–500 msFor lyrics only
Bluetooth audio output150–300 msNo (for monitoring)
Wired headphones + HDMI video50–100 msYes

Best practice: Video to TV via HDMI (or wireless), audio to wired headphones or a PA system. Don't monitor through Bluetooth or TV speakers if you're singing seriously.

Cable and Adapter Recommendations

  • Android: USB-C to HDMI adapter (Anker, UGREEN, or official brand)
  • iPhone/iPad: Lightning Digital AV Adapter (Apple) or USB-C to HDMI (iPad Pro)
  • HDMI cable: High-speed, 6 feet or shorter
  • Audio: 3.5mm to 1/4" or RCA for PA systems; USB audio interface for professional setups

KANTA Settings for External Displays

Go to More → Settings → Stage & Display:

  • External display: Enable dual-screen mode
  • Lyrics font size: Increase for TV viewing (24–32 pt)
  • Background visuals: Enable for stage ambiance
  • Audio routing: Choose output device
  • Microphone monitor: Enable if using headphones, disable if using PA (avoid feedback)
